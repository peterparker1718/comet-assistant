## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Indonesia is the **United States' 10th-largest import partner**, with total US goods imports from Indonesia reaching **$28.1–$35 billion in 2024** depending on measurement methodology, creating a **$17.9 billion US trade deficit**. Indonesia is a critical, multi-sector supplier across electronics, apparel, footwear, rubber, seafood, furniture, and critical minerals.

**Key 2025 Development:** On April 2, 2025, President Trump imposed a **32% reciprocal tariff** on all Indonesian goods as part of "Liberation Day" tariff actions — citing Indonesia's high non-tariff barriers and $17.9B US trade deficit. After negotiations, a **July 2025 US-Indonesia trade deal reduced the rate to 19%**, with Indonesia eliminating barriers on 99%+ of US products and agreeing to cooperate on critical minerals and supply chain security.

**Net impact on US importers:** $361M+ in additional annual import costs at the 19% rate vs. pre-tariff baseline. Most exposed sectors: electronics ($4.8B), apparel/footwear ($2-3B), furniture ($1.4B), seafood ($1.9B), rubber ($1.7B).

## US–INDONESIA TRADE DEAL SUMMARY (July 2025)

**White House Fact Sheet Key Points:**
- Indonesia pays US reciprocal tariff rate: **19%** (down from 32%)
- Indonesia eliminates tariffs on **99%+ of US products** on preferential basis
- Indonesia removes restrictions on exports of all industrial commodities including **critical minerals**
- Both nations cooperate on **supply chain resilience, duty evasion prevention, and export controls**
- US agriculture gains expanded access to Indonesian market (Indonesia was 11th-largest US ag export market at $2.9B in 2024)

**Net Assessment:** The deal is positive for both sides but does not eliminate cost pressure. US importers now face a permanent 19% tariff floor on Indonesian goods where MFN rates were previously 0–5%, representing a significant structural cost increase of 14–19 percentage points across most categories.
